        rho = 0.70, marginal EVI 0.25, so the CONDITIONAL EVI is 0.1275

n = 500
   pool_predictive (free scale per hour)      bias -0.0239  sd 0.0573  RMSE 0.0617
   standardise (q0.50/q0.90, threshold 60%)   bias +0.0164  sd 0.0820  RMSE 0.0832

n = 2000
   pool_predictive (free scale per hour)      bias -0.0212  sd 0.0339  RMSE 0.0398
   standardise (q0.50/q0.90, threshold 60%)   bias -0.0149  sd 0.0439  RMSE 0.0460

Read the ordering off the numbers above rather than assuming one: which
estimator has less bias depends on the sample size and on how each is set
up, and they are close on RMSE throughout.

The mechanism that decides it: the standardised route has to estimate each
hour's normalising quantiles, and when those are themselves noisy the
error propagates into z and inflates the fitted shape. That is worst at
small n, where a conditional holds few points. The pooled route has the
opposite failing -- a free scale per hour, each informed by a handful of
tail points, which biases its shared shape low as cells get thin.

So the case for the standardised route is not RMSE. It is that the pooled
values are one per observation instead of a re-weighting of the same
responses many times over, so its likelihood is a real one: the standard
error printed alongside it means what it usually means, and likelihood
ratio intervals and tests are available. The shared-shape fit needs a
bootstrap for the same thing.


=====================================================================
PART 2  Setting up the standardisation
=====================================================================

n = 2000. Which quantiles define location and scale:
   location q0.50, scale gap to q0.90         bias -0.0081  sd 0.0453  RMSE 0.0458
   location q0.70, scale gap to q0.95         bias -0.0013  sd 0.0390  RMSE 0.0388
   location q0.80, scale gap to q0.98         bias +0.0177  sd 0.0470  RMSE 0.0499

   The middle pair wins. Too high and the normaliser is itself a tail
   estimate, which is the thing being avoided; too low and it stops
   describing the part of the distribution the fit is about.

n = 2000. Where the POT threshold goes, on the standardised scale:
   threshold at the 60% point of z            bias -0.0093  sd 0.0382  RMSE 0.0391
   threshold at the 75% point of z            bias -0.0049  sd 0.0537  RMSE 0.0536
   threshold at the 90% point of z            bias -0.0088  sd 0.0823  RMSE 0.0822

   Raising the threshold buys no reliable improvement in bias here and
   costs a great deal of variance, so the low end of the usual range is
   the right side to err on.
