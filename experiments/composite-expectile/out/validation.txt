=== 1. The loss functions target what they claim ===
Minimising the sample loss at a single level p should return that level's
expectile (L2) or quantile (L1). Note the absolute value in the expectile
loss: without it the criterion is unbounded below.

p=0.30  argmin |p-I|(y-e)^2 =   0.3536  (true expectile   0.3513)
        argmin (p-I)(y-e)^2 = 9999.9998  <- no absolute value: runs to the boundary
        argmin (p-I)(y-q)   =  -0.1828  (true quantile   -0.1822)
p=0.70  argmin |p-I|(y-e)^2 =   1.4083  (true expectile   1.4032)
        argmin (p-I)(y-e)^2 = 9999.9998  <- no absolute value: runs to the boundary
        argmin (p-I)(y-q)   =   1.1462  (true quantile    1.1449)
p=0.95  argmin |p-I|(y-e)^2 =   3.5066  (true expectile   3.4881)
        argmin (p-I)(y-e)^2 = 9999.9998  <- no absolute value: runs to the boundary
        argmin (p-I)(y-q)   =   4.0766  (true quantile    4.0564)
